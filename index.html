Approach

How I work

Test by risk

Each change gets the approach its risk calls for: exploratory, regression, functional or backend. One recipe for everything wastes time where it matters least.

API before UI

Business rules are checked at the service level, where tests are fast and stable. The browser covers what only a browser can prove.

A red build means something

Flaky tests get isolated and fixed, and pipelines own their environment, so a failure points to the code and not to a broken server.

Case studies

Infosis · Mobile food-ordering app

Catching payment failures before users do

Context

I was part of a QA automation team maintaining three frameworks in parallel: a mobile food-ordering app (Appium, Rest Assured), a cashierless supermarket platform for a Spanish chain where shoppers scan and pay from their phone (Playwright), and the ZEUS ERP (Java, Selenium, Cucumber).

Problem

Payments through third-party APIs, such as Mercado Pago and bank integrations, kept breaking after new deployments. Every incident meant re-checking the flows by hand.

What I did
  • Proposed moving coverage to the backend: an API regression suite with Rest Assured that runs after every deployment.
  • Presented the strategy and its results to the CTO, the QA Lead and the Head of Operations.
  • Isolated the flaky tests that kept crashing the virtualized Android emulator in the mobile suite.

Payment regressions checked automatically on every deployment instead of by hand, and GitLab CI pipeline time down 20%.

Globant · North American financial institution

A regression suite 30% faster

Context

Dicsys · Agribusiness exporter

Testing by risk across a grain exporter’s systems

Context

Internal systems for one of Argentina’s leading agribusiness companies: accounting, tax settlement driven by new regulations, and a transport system for the trains, trucks and ships that move grain to the company’s own port. Work arrived as requirements, from a change in a tax formula to a full migration.

Problem

Each requirement carried a different risk, so a single testing approach did not fit them all. The newest system, built with Angular and Node, had no automation.

What I did
  • Chose the approach per requirement and acted as Business Analyst between the business areas and the developers.
  • Started the Playwright + JavaScript E2E suite for the transport system from scratch.
  • Paired with a manual-QA teammate, using VS Code and Copilot to generate test cases, so she could take her first steps in automation.
  • Automated regression scenarios with GeneXus Recorder.

Manual regression effort down an estimated 35%, and an E2E suite in place for the transport system.
